How the plan is built, and what the test showed
  • Signal: a day closes above the coin's highest close of the previous 55 days.
  • Entry: that day's closing price. Daily candles close at 00:00 UTC. A signal is only real once the day has closed.
  • Stop: the lowest close of the 20 days before the signal. It is a real order sitting in the market, and it is usually far away (20–30% on average), which is why the position has to be small.
  • Target: entry plus the stop distance multiplied by the style you picked. "R" means one stop distance: +1R is a gain equal to what you risked.
  • Position size: your account × risk % ÷ the stop distance %. With a 25% stop and 1% risk the position is 4% of the account. No leverage is needed.
  • Bitcoin filter: a signal only counts on a day Bitcoin closes above its own 200-day average. Since 2022 the signals this filter rejects won 47% and lost money; the ones it keeps won 65%. When Bitcoin is below the average there will be no new signals at all, sometimes for months (all of 2022).
  • US stocks: the same rule on 40 large US stocks and 12 index funds, tested from 2005. Their signals appear after the US close (about 21:00 UTC, weekdays) and prices on those cards update at each scan, not every second. About half of the stock result comes from the stock market's own long rise; random entry days made +0.12R against +0.23R for breakout days. The 40 stocks are today's giants, which is hindsight. Stops are closer (about 10%), so positions are larger: about 10% of the account at 1% risk.
  • Dip rule (its own tab): a US stock or fund that is above its 200-day average closes below its lower band (20-day average minus 2 standard deviations). On a day when the whole market is down (the S&P 500 more than 1.5 standard deviations below its own 20-day average) a smaller dip of 1 standard deviation also counts. Entry is that close. The stop is 3 average daily ranges below, about 7%. The exit is the first later daily close above the 20-day average, or 20 trading days. Tested on 583 stocks and funds from 2005; the figures are at the top of the dip tab. Winners are smaller than losers, and about 1 trade in 4 hits the full stop. Signals come in bursts: about a third of days have none, and on a market-wide dip day there can be hundreds. The single-company list is today's S&P 500, which is hindsight.
  • Market-wide and stock-only dips: the same dip signal did better when the whole market was down with the stock: 72% won, averaging +1.1% of the position from 2016 on. When only the stock was falling and the market was not, 64% won and the average was +0.4%. Market-wide dip days are about 1 day in 8, with dozens of signals each, so an account limited to 10 positions can take only a few of them. A further variant with the stop 5 ranges away won 75% but loses about 11% of the position when it fails; it is in the test files, not on this page.
  • MEXC: a card marked MEXC means that stock is listed there as a perpetual futures contract. The test used real stock closing prices with no leverage. A futures contract adds funding payments, its own price, and liquidation if leverage is used; none of that was tested.
  • One plan at a time per coin: as in the test, a coin gets a new plan only on a breakout day that comes after its previous plan has finished.
  • Late entries: the test bought at the signal day's close. If price has since moved more than a quarter of the stop distance above entry, the card says so, because that is no longer the trade that was tested.
  • The scanner follows the 40 most heavily traded coins. The rule was tested on 380: it held up on large coins and failed on small ones (about zero since 2022, a loss since 2024), so small coins are left out. The 40 were picked by today's trading volume, which is hindsight and flatters the numbers a little.
  • Since 2022 the edge is much thinner than before (about a third of the long-run average). Expect long flat stretches.
  • Signals arrive in clusters, because coins move together. Ten open plans are not ten independent bets; a market drop can stop most of them at once.
  • Only coins that still trade today were tested. Dead coins are missing, which makes every number here look better than it was.

Prices: Binance spot for crypto; Yahoo Finance daily data for US stocks (updated at each scan, not live). This page places no trades and is not financial advice. It shows the levels a tested rule produces; whether to act on them, and with how much, is your decision. Past results do not guarantee future results.
